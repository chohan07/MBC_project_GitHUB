[SC01] How did ancient Koreans tell exact time 600 years ago without modern clocks?
[SC02] Today, we simply glance at our phones, but Joseon Dynasty Koreans had a surprisingly advanced system.
[SC03] During the daytime, people checked Angbuilgu, a public sundial shaped like a concave cauldron placed in busy streets.
[SC04] It even featured animal drawings of the zodiac so ordinary people who couldn't read Hanja could easily tell the hour.
[SC05] On cloudy days or at night, the automatic water clock Jagyeongnu took over.
[SC06] Invented by King Sejong and Jang Yeong-sil, rising water triggered iron balls that prompted mechanical wooden figures to strike drums and bells precisely on time.
[SC07] This precise time was relayed across the capital by the grand bell pavilion, signaling the opening and closing of city gates and guiding daily routines.
