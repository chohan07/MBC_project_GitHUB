3. 영어 전체 대본 :
How did ancient Koreans tell exact time 600 years ago without modern clocks? Today, we simply glance at our phones, but Joseon Dynasty Koreans had a surprisingly advanced system. During the daytime, people checked Angbuilgu, a public sundial shaped like a concave cauldron placed in busy streets. It even featured animal drawings of the zodiac so ordinary people who couldn't read Hanja could easily tell the hour. On cloudy days or at night, the automatic water clock Jagyeongnu took over. Invented by King Sejong and Jang Yeong-sil, rising water triggered iron balls that prompted mechanical wooden figures to strike drums and bells precisely on time. This precise time was relayed across the capital by the grand bell pavilion, signaling the opening and closing of city gates and guiding daily routines. Then, could Joseon citizens freely walk the streets once the curfew bell rang at night?
